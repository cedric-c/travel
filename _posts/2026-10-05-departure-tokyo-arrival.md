The 14.5-hour flight to Tokyo wasn’t so bad either. I used to find it harder to sit through. These days, the toughest part is the last stretch, around an hour before they serve the final meal, when I’m ready to be anywhere other than my seat.

I booked a hotel in Kichijoji on a dear friend’s recommendation. Good call: I’m about 200 feet from the station entrance, with plenty to explore nearby. Getting there from Haneda Airport took about an hour and a half.

![Kichijoji after arriving in Tokyo]({{ '/images/kichijoji1-2026-10-05.jpeg' | relative_url }})

![Exploring Kichijoji]({{ '/images/kichijoji2-2026-10-05.jpeg' | relative_url }})

After dropping off my bags, I went exploring and stumbled onto Inokashira Park. Scenic paths, plenty of benches, and young couples occupying most of them. I think I’ve found my spot for morning walks.

By then, the United Airlines chicken sandwich I’d eaten on the plane--one-third of which was empty--was wearing off. I found a shop selling baozi and decided to put my Mandarin to work.

Naturally, my first attempt at ordering food in Mandarin on this trip happened in Japan. "Liù niúròu bāozi." One small flaw in the plan: the person taking my order needed to speak Mandarin. The Chinese chain’s signage had given me more confidence than it should have. Rookie mistake.

![The meat bun shop where my Mandarin ordering attempt failed]({{ '/images/failed-baozi-purchase-2026-10-05.jpeg' | relative_url }})

On my robot friend’s recommendation, I made my way to Harmonica Yokocho: a cluster of little alleys packed with bars and restaurants. Great atmosphere.

![An evening in Harmonica Yokocho]({{ '/images/harmonica-yokocho-2026-10-05.jpeg' | relative_url }})

At a bar, I met a Frenchman who works in the travel industry and has lived in Asia for twenty-five years. We talked about technology, AI, and how much China had changed since his visits in the early ’90s. He remembered Shenzhen as a small fishing village--a striking contrast with the city I’ve visited. We both agreed that the pace of China’s growth was impressive.

Once we’d settled in, I did what I tend to do and steered the conversation toward politics. Apparently that’s a bit taboo. I’d read the room and figured we’d be fine, which we were. I mostly listened and kept things light.

I spent the rest of the evening wandering through shops until around 8 p.m. I didn’t find what I was looking for, so a visit to Ginza Six is on the list for the coming days.

By 8 p.m., I’d found a park, failed to order baozi, and discussed politics with a stranger. A respectable first day.
