Espresso tonic is one of my favourite refreshing drinks. It’s also surprisingly divisive. Apparently, mixing bitter coffee with bitter tonic water isn’t everyone’s idea of a good time. Who would have thought?

Although, to be fair, there’s usually a sweet citrus syrup thrown into the mix to balance out the bitterness.

I was first introduced to this rather peculiar concoction some three years ago, of all places, at a KFC. Not exactly where you’d expect to discover your next favourite coffee drink.

![My first espresso tonic]({{ '/images/kcoffee-2024-09-03-2.jpeg' | relative_url }})

But it worked, and I’ve been a fan ever since.

Then again, some of my favourite foods are combinations that sound downright questionable on paper.

Take jam and cheese on toast, for example.

I was introduced to this rather unusual combination by my grandfather some 20 years ago, when I spent a week at my grandparents’ house during summer break.

I’d developed a habit of waking up rather late every morning, so breakfast would often turn into lunch. My grandfather would always offer to make me something to eat.

One morning, he asked if I wanted jam on toast. Sure, why not?

Then he asked if I wanted cheese on it.

Cheese? On jam toast? What an odd combination.

But I figured I’d give it a try. And, well, it worked. So much so that I ended up having it every morning for the rest of the week.

Then there’s the idea of eating noodles for breakfast. Something I originally found rather odd, until I discovered Wuhanese hot-dry noodles. Nothing quite dispels that notion like a good bowl of 热干面 (règānmiàn).

And, of course, there’s cilantro. Not really an unusual combination of anything. It just exists, minding its own business, somehow managing to divide the entire population.

I suppose the lesson here is that some things are worth trying before deciding they’re terrible.

And if you still hate espresso tonic, well, more for me.
