I started my day by ordering a DiDi (Chinese Uber) to take me to the train station.

Naturally, every other car in Hong Kong is a Tesla, and naturally, the one I ordered was silver. So when a silver Tesla pulled up, I did what any reasonable person would do: I hopped in.

I popped the trunk, feeling rather pleased with myself for helping the driver load my luggage, when I noticed a woman getting out of the passenger seat.

*Wow. Efficient.*

Had DiDi somehow solved a travelling salesman problem I didn’t know about? Was this some sort of revolutionary ride-sharing optimization? Genius!

Anyway, there I was, opening this guy’s trunk and starting to load my luggage when the driver stepped out and gave me the most bewildered look imaginable.

I checked the licence plate.

Wrong car.

An excellent start.

My *actual* DiDi eventually arrived, and we were off. Naturally, I decided to put my Mandarin to work.

"Nǐ hǎo! Wǒ jiào Cédric. Wǒ láizì Jiānádà, Wòtàihuá."

*Hello! My name is Cédric. I’m from Ottawa, Canada.*

And then he hit me with something along the lines of:

"Nǐ kàn qǐlái xiàng Měiguórén."

*You look like an American.*

Whoa, whoa, whoa. Hold up.

*Nǐ kàn qǐlái* -- okay, something about looking. That whole chunk in the middle? No clue. But *Měiguórén*?

**That**, I know.

Did this guy just tell me I look like an American?

Listen, pal. I may look like an American. I may love American movies. I may occasionally eat at McDonald’s. I may be the biggest Apple fanboy this side of the Pacific.

But an **American**?

No way, José.

"Wǒ shì Jiānádà rén!"

*I’m Canadian!*

Anyway, he was cool about it. So was I. No harm, no foul.

At some point, I told him:

"Wǒ jīntiān yào qù Chóngqìng."

*I’m going to Chongqing today.*

Now, I don’t know what it is about this particular sentence, but every time I say it, people look at me as though I’ve suddenly switched to English.

Is it the tones? Am I pronouncing Chongqing wrong? Do I have something stuck in my teeth?

So I pulled out my phone, typed the sentence into a translator, and let the robot do the talking.

The robot said what sounded to me like **exactly the same thing**.

"Ohhhhhhh!" went the driver.

Just like the fifty people before him.

It’s the tones, isn’t it?

⸻

The seven-hour train ride to Chongqing, however, turned out to be one of the highlights of the day, and I have Finn to thank for that.

Finn is from Xi’an, and we spent most of the journey talking about just about everything: love, relationships, money, family, and life in general. We exchanged stories, cracked jokes, and somehow managed to fill seven hours without running out of things to talk about.

We also talked about family. Finn told me that he takes responsibility for looking after his parents, since his brother isn’t quite wired that way. I thought that said a lot about the kind of person he is.

Finn was funny, thoughtful, and genuinely interesting to talk to. Between the conversations, the jokes, and the occasional bit of unsolicited wisdom, seven hours somehow felt like two.

And when we finally arrived in Chongqing, he even insisted on paying for our cab ride.

What a guy.

If I ever had to take a transatlantic flight and could choose one person to sit next to, purely to optimize for the perception of time passing, Finn would be my guy.

I really hope our paths cross again. Xi’an is still on my list of places to visit, and now I’ve got one more reason to go.
