teamLab Borderless was quite the experience. It's basically an enormous indoor light show where digital art is projected across entire rooms, walls, floors, and pretty much anything else they can find.

I would definitely recommend it, and in all honesty, I'd consider it a must-see if you're in Tokyo.

Some of the rooms use projectors to completely transform the surfaces around you, while others use mirrors and suspended lights to create what looks like an endless sea of light stretching off into infinity. The effect works because the floors, ceilings, and walls are all mirrors, so after a while it becomes genuinely difficult to tell where the room actually ends.

![teamLab Borderless]({{ '/images/borderless-c1-2026-10-06.jpeg' | relative_url }})

My favourite room by far was Asymmetric Universe. It uses an arrangement of projectors positioned around a tunnel-like space, firing actual beams of light through the air. The beams shift, intersect, and form geometric patterns around you, making it feel like you're standing inside the light itself. It's hard to properly capture in a photo because so much of the experience comes from watching the beams move through the space, but standing inside it was probably the highlight of the entire exhibit for me.

Some of the rooms also reminded me a lot of the tesseract scene from Interstellar, when Cooper enters the higher-dimensional space near the end of the movie. Obviously there are fewer black holes involved here, but the same idea of light and space repeating seemingly forever immediately came to mind.

There are also a few areas where the projections continue right across the edges and corners of the room, which helps sell the illusion that what you're looking at isn't confined to a flat wall.

![Projection wrapping around the edges of the room]({{ '/images/borderless-edges1-2026-10-06.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Near the end of the exhibit there's a tea house, which I'd recommend stopping at. Even the tea is apparently not allowed to just be tea here.

Once they serve it, projections react to the cup itself. A flower appears in the tea, and when you pick up or move the cup, its petals scatter across the table before another flower eventually appears. It's a small thing compared with some of the massive installations outside, but it was a pretty great way to end the experience.
