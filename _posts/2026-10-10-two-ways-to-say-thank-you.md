## Breakfast, with limited human interaction

Ms. Yoyo from the hotel had recommended a breakfast spot, so I put it to the test. Let me tell you, she did not let me down.

I'm finally ready to try my hand at ordering when the server approaches, hands me a receipt with a QR code, and says, “Order here.”

Had technology gone too far? Had human interaction been reduced to an endocrine-disrupting slip of paper? Why does this milk tea taste so good?

Breakfast was good. My ability to judge how much breakfast I needed was less good.

The siu mai were hot, juicy, and salty. Very satisfying to bite into. The pork buns were a little too sweet for my taste; I prefer the savoury kind. The steamed rice rolls in Sichuan sauce, though, were a hit: cilantro, spice, and enough heat to make me reconsider my choices.

It was already 25°C, and I was in full pants-stick-to-legs sweats. On top of that, Hong Kong had a Yellow Fire Danger Warning in effect.

It's like they knew I couldn't restrain myself.

## Food? Me?

By the time I finished at the museum, I was hungry again. I made my way to Central and began walking up and down the alleys in search of lunch.

![A narrow market alley in Central]({{ '/images/alleywalk1-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

There were vendors on either side, with people moving in both directions through a gap about as wide as an escalator. Whenever a vendor stepped out to tend to the front of a stall, we lost a little more of the available x-axis. Every step required some deliberation.

My robot friend had recommended a restaurant with a lineup, but it didn't accept Visa. An apparently recurring theme. The hunt continued, although not for very long: someone flagged me down and said, “Food.”

Food? Me? Well, why didn't you say it sooner!

That's right, it was a reverse cab hail, and I was fully participating.

She sat me at the only table with another foreigner: Minh, a student from Germany finishing his degree and spending a month here on a trip combining school and work.

![Lunch and milk tea in Central]({{ '/images/lunch-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Lunch was good. So good, in fact, that it hadn't crossed my mind to check whether *this* restaurant accepted Visa.

It did not.

Thankfully, Minh kindly covered the meal. I had really overestimated Visa's prevalence and underestimated good old cash. Or octomoney.

We ended up spending the rest of the afternoon together, stopping at Decathlon and grabbing some dessert. I forgot what it was called, but finding the shop was a mission in itself. Surely some Chongqing foreshadowing.

![Dessert during my afternoon with Minh]({{ '/images/dessert1-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

We finished our afternoon with a quick rest at a temple.

![The street on our walk to the temple]({{ '/images/templeWalk1-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

![The temple entrance]({{ '/images/templeWalk2-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Minh also solved a mystery I'd been wondering about since arriving in Hong Kong: why does it sometimes rain beside a building but not three feet farther into the street? Full-on drops hitting me square in the glasses. Weird. I could've sworn there was no rain in the forecast.

Apparently, it's water dripping from the air conditioners in the high-rises.

Yum.

Minh, big thanks for the afternoon and the meal. I'm glad we got to chat and that I got to hear about your time in Hong Kong and elsewhere.

## The good old-fashioned way up

After an afternoon of walking around Central, I decided the next sensible activity was more walking. Uphill, this time: Victoria Peak.

![The start of the walk up to Victoria Peak]({{ '/images/hike2-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

![The road climbing toward the Peak]({{ '/images/hike1-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

Early in the hike, I met Leo and his girlfriend, Seeki. Another pair of brave souls taking the good old-fashioned route to the top.

Leo and I initially bonded over our shared suffering. Soon, though, we were talking about technology, AI, politics, Java and Python, BRICS, geopolitics, petrodollars and treasuries, and Tesla's cost structure. The usual small talk on a steep hill.

Leo works as a fund manager and previously worked at Goldman Sachs, so I had plenty of questions. It was probably a little unfair to him: he had come out for a hike, and I was picking his brain about his work. Seeki eventually stepped in to ask whether we wanted a break.

Speaking of Seeki, she deserves a mention for being an absolute trooper. She made sure we all got to the top and never broke a sweat. She's got to be hiding a second pair of lungs or something. Meanwhile, I was at “just got out of the shower” levels of perspiration.

The conversation carried us up the hill, moving between the pros and cons of different forms of government and whatever else came to mind. We finally reached the top, where we spent some time enjoying the view and taking pictures.

## A first, for someone else

We went to an Indian restaurant at the top. Here's something I hadn't expected: Seeki had never tried Indian food before. Not once.

![Dinner together at the Peak]({{ '/images/lateSnack-2026-10-10.jpeg' | relative_url }})

I was happy to be there for it. I'd been spending this trip discovering things that were new to me. Now I was watching someone else discover something I'd been familiar with for years.

Of course, she made the rookie mistake of biting directly into a hot pepper. We've all made that mistake once.

Maybe you get to experience something for the first time twice: once yourself, and again through someone else's eyes. I hadn't expected Indian food to be one of my favourite memories of Hong Kong, but here we are.

Leo also treated me to a beer and a tart, and covered the cab ride back down. We'd only met a few hours earlier.

Seeki also taught me my first words in Cantonese: two common ways to say thank you, depending on what you're thanking someone for.

- **唔該 (*m4 goi1*)** — for help or service.
- **多謝 (*do1 ze6*)** — for a gift or something someone gives you.

The pronunciation is written in Jyutping, with the numbers marking the tones. Different from the Mandarin Pinyin I've been learning.

A useful lesson after all that generosity. I'd started the morning trying to order breakfast through a QR code and ended it sharing dinner with people I'd only just met.

So, to Minh, Leo, and Seeki:

Thank you. Merci.
